    About this model and sources

    This is a simulation only. Never make or change a rocket motor. Real model rockets are launched only with me, using certified store-bought motors and the NAR Model Rocket Safety Code.

    Simplified model: 2-D flight (up and downrange) stepped every 0.002 s with Newton's second law. Constant thrust for the burn time; propellant mass burns off. Drag = ½ × air density × speed² × drag coefficient × area, opposite the motion; bigger fins add mass and drag; streamer or parachute add drag only after they open at apogee (all recovery choices have the same packed mass). CG from part masses and positions (at liftoff); CP from a simplified Barrowman method (nose cone + 3 fins). A turning effect about CG grows with speed squared, so fins do little when the rocket is slow. A damaged fin adds a constant one-sided turning effect. Launch rail 1 m. Max tilt is measured from leaving the rail until 1 s after burnout. The drawing exaggerates small wobbles (angle to the airflow, up to about 7°) 3× so you can see them. In 3D the rocket and force arrows are drawn larger than true scale. Generic motors only; impulse class from standard total-impulse bands. No wind.

    Real model rocket motors fire an ejection charge after a set delay (the last number in a code like C6-5); this lab simply opens recovery exactly at apogee.

    Overstable: a very high margin (roughly above 2 cal) is still safe, but in wind a real rocket turns into the wind (weathercocks). This lab has no wind, so it can't show that.

    Sources: NASA Glenn Beginner's Guide to Aeronautics: Model Rockets and Real Rockets (www1.grc.nasa.gov/beginners-guide-to-aeronautics/model-rockets-and-real-rockets/), Rocket Center of Gravity (…/rocket-center-of-gravity/), Conditions for Rocket Stability (…/conditions-for-rocket-stability/), Rocket Thrust (…/rocket-thrust/), Flight of an Air Rocket (…/flight-of-an-air-rocket/). Center of pressure: Barrowman, J. S., "The Practical Calculation of the Aerodynamic Characteristics of Slender Finned Vehicles" (1967), simplified.
